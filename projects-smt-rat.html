Does a set of polynomial inequalities over the real numbers have a solution? SMT-RAT answers questions like this from modules that can be combined into a solver — I worked on the one that narrows the search space with interval arithmetic.

  • The problem

    Many questions in verification and engineering boil down to one: is there an assignment of real numbers that satisfies a Boolean combination of polynomial constraints? Can this hybrid system reach an unsafe state, does this rewrite system terminate, does this inequality hold? Such formulas of non-linear real arithmetic are decidable, but the complete methods are expensive — at least exponential in the worst case.

  • SMT-RAT

    SMT solvers combine a fast SAT solver for the Boolean structure with theory solvers for the arithmetic. SMT-RAT (Satisfiability-Modulo-Theories Real Algebra Toolbox), developed at RWTH’s Theory of Hybrid Systems group, makes the theory part modular: each method — simplex, virtual substitution, interval constraint propagation, cylindrical algebraic decomposition and more — is a module, and a strategy chains modules into a solver. Cheap, incomplete methods go first and hand a smaller problem to the expensive, complete ones. The toolbox is open source, reads SMT-LIB 2 and builds on the CArL library for polynomials.

  • Interval constraint propagation

    Interval constraint propagation (ICP) works on boxes of intervals, one per variable. It uses each constraint to cut away parts of the box that cannot contain a solution — contraction — until the box is empty (unsatisfiable) or small enough to hand on. Its weak spot is interval arithmetic itself: it does not know that two occurrences of x are the same number. That dependency problem makes results wider than necessary (figure above), so ICP contracts less and needs more iterations.

  • My contribution

    I improved SMT-RAT’s ICP module in two ways [Net15]. First, a new contraction method — interval propagation through the constraint — in addition to the existing Newton-based contraction. Second, a data structure for multivariate Horner schemes: polynomials are rewritten in nested form, which needs fewer operations and repeats variables less often, so the intervals stay tighter. Two heuristics choose the variable to factor out next — one fast, one aimed at the smallest over-approximation — and a simplification step compresses the result.

    Evaluated on the standard SMT-LIB benchmark sets, the propagation clearly paid off: on keymaera (hybrid-systems verification) more instances were solved with fewer timeouts in little more than half the time, and on hong nearly twice as many test cases could be solved in the same time frame. The Horner schemes did make ICP more precise — up to 46 % fewer contractions on some instances — but building them cost more time than the tighter intervals saved. An honest result that pointed to the next steps: choosing the heuristic per input, and evaluating polynomials from the perspective of a single variable.
